# Learning science for a short, sleep-deprived cram

> Review note: DOIs for Yoo 2007 (nn1851), Dunlosky 2013, and Cowan 2001 match known papers. The PNAS DOI (Bastani et al. 2025) is not checked against the source.

## 1. Learning while sleep-deprived
- One night without sleep cut hippocampal activity during encoding, and later recall was worse. Effort alone did not make up for it. ([Yoo et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1851))
- Lapses in sustained attention are the largest effect of sleep loss. Sleep loss also makes performance fall faster within each task block (time-on-task). ([Lim & Dinges 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18591490/); [meta-analysis 2010](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/LimDinges2010MetaAnalysis.pdf))
- Caffeine improves vigilance and reaction time during sleep loss. Dose and timing matter, and it harms the sleep that follows. ([Irwin et al. 2019](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/0456765f-9af8-4497-82e2-7a431695209c))
- A 1-hour nap after study helped memory as much as another hour of cramming, and the nap's advantage lasted a week. ([Cousins et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30371902/))
- Studying in place of sleep was linked to more academic problems the next day. (observational) ([Gillen-O'Neel et al. 2013](https://www.russellsage.org/node/4546))

## 2. Cramming in a few hours
- Practice testing has high utility. Rereading and highlighting have low utility. ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266))
- Retrieval practice improves learning (g of about 0.6-0.7). Multiple-choice practice works, and practice that matches the exam format helps a little more. ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/))
- Errors made with high confidence are the most likely to be fixed after feedback (hypercorrection). This helps review that focuses on errors. ([Hypercorrection review](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/d6ab09ca-f55c-4efb-9d32-2788bc5838b2))
- Interleaving helps more when categories are similar and easy to confuse (g of about 0.4). It does not help with word lists. ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/))
- Spacing helps, and the best gap grows with the time until the test. Few studies tested very short gaps. (weak evidence for spacing within one day) ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/))
- Elaborative "why" questions have moderate utility. ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266))

## 3. Learning a lot quickly
- Prioritize. Rereading material the learner already knows gives little gain, so time should go to tested weak spots. ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266))
- Words plus visuals have a positive average effect on retention, but the benefit depends on the design. Decorative visuals do not help. ([Dual coding meta-analysis](https://www.ambition.org.uk/when-and-how-to-use-dual-coding-effectively-in-teaching/); [multiple representations](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09958-y))
- Working memory holds about 4 chunks, so each card should carry one contrast. ([Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922))
- The keyword mnemonic has low utility in general. Use it only for a few arbitrary facts. (weak evidence) ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266))

## 4. Using AI well
- A structured AI tutor beat in-class active learning, with more learning in less time. No delayed test was given. ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6))
- Unguided GPT-4 raised practice scores, but exam scores fell 17% once access was removed. A tutor that gave hints, not answers, avoided the harm. ([Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122))
- Using an LLM in place of your own work (having it produce solutions) reduces understanding. Using it alongside your work (asking for explanations) improves understanding. LLM use also inflates how much people think they learned. ([Lehmann et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047))
- Questions generated by AI before study helped learning more than reading or studying an AI-made outline. (weak evidence) ([Pan et al. 2025](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PSTIW_2025.pdf))

## 5. Multiple-choice strategy
- Changing an answer more often fixes it than breaks it. Students wrongly believe the reverse (the first-instinct fallacy). ([Kruger et al. 2005](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/teaching/myth-its-better-to-stick-to-your-first-impulse-than-go-back-and-change-multiple-choice-test-answers.html))
- Changing a low-confidence guess helps more than changing a confident answer. ([Couchman et al. 2016, summary](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2022/2/24-1))
- After a first wrong choice, later choices are still above chance, so eliminating options captures partial knowledge. (weak evidence) ([Elimination testing](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/modeling-partial-knowledge-on-multiple-choice-items-using-elimination-testing/812768929012252674-7571))
- Cues such as "the longest answer" or "all of the above" come from practitioner advice only. Do not rely on them. (weak evidence) ([Test-wise guide](https://intranet.missouriwestern.edu/advising/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2018/09/Test-Wise-Strategies.pdf))

## Rules for our session
1. **Blocks:** drill for 15-20 min, then take a 3-5 min break away from screens. The exact length is our inference from time-on-task effects. (1.2)
2. **Question first, always:** the learner commits to an answer and a confidence level before seeing anything. Then show the correct answer and one line on why the trap works. (2.1, 2.2, 4.2)
3. **Error-only content:** build every item from missed practice questions. Order high-confidence misses first. (2.3, 3.1)
4. **Contrast cards:** pair confusable concepts side by side in one small visual table, with at most 4 items per card. Mix the pairs together. (2.4, 3.2, 3.3)
5. **Re-test:** ask each missed item again at the end of its block and in a final mixed round. (2.5, 2.1)
6. **Body:** take caffeine once, early. Choose sleep (or a 20-60 min nap after the main drill) over a last hour of cramming. (1.3, 1.4, 1.5)
7. **AI role:** Claude writes the questions and the feedback and never gives an answer before the learner has committed. (4.1-4.3)
8. **Exam:** eliminate wrong options first and flag unsure items. When reviewing, change an answer if you find a concrete reason, especially for guesses. (5.1-5.3)
